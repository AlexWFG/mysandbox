# Between Capital and the Asset — explainer storyboard

Slug `capital-and-asset` · source deck `Propchain — Between Capital and the Asset.pdf` (strategy note, working draft, September 2026).
Target runtime ≈ 3:45 · 1920×1080 · 30 fps · narrator George (series voice).

## What the viewer must understand

1. **The setting.** About twenty parties sit between capital and the asset. Two businesses work that stretch: Prop.com (outcome services, cash now) and Propchain (the rails, a standard everyone runs on). For each party the question is *own it, tool it, or onboard it* — and in what order.
2. **The rule.** Every party splits into an **act** (carries liability, stays independent → *we tool it*), a **process** (desk work, most of the hours and the fee → *a Prop.com service*) and **data** (consumed and emitted → *Propchain attests it*). Worked example: the valuer. Why: independence is the product.
3. **The map.** Eight layers in the order a deal moves. Every party gets one verb: **Onboard** the participants (the market), **Tool** the independents, **Serve** the process half.
4. **Prop.com's order.** Sell the outcome of a desk, not the AI. Five criteria score the lines → three tiers: Now (analyst desks, no licence), Next (licensed desk, under a partner's act), Later (field work, by integration). Land and expand inside one client.
5. **Propchain's order.** Sequenced by what each party unlocks: lenders → valuers → notaries → assurance & peers → source & finality. Dependency, not rank: a notary tool with nothing to settle is a demo.
6. **Why it holds together.** Tools for independents are free or near-free and never produce the act; services make records, records make rails, rails create demand for services; Prop.com is only a participant on the rails.

## Arc

Title → the crowd in the middle → the two businesses and the question → the rule (act / process / data, valuer example) → the map (8 layers, 3 verbs) → Prop.com's tiers → Propchain's sequence (with the notary "demo vs closing" proof) → tools + flywheel + neutrality → "Own the desk. Tool the act. Onboard the market." → end card.

## Decisions

- Slides 08–09 (seat table, scoring) are compressed into one "seats fold into a desk" beat and a criteria sweep; exact qualitative ratings are not read out (they are "qualitative and relative", per the deck).
- The deck's own verbs are used for the map (Onboard / Tool / Serve); "own it" appears only in the framing question (slide 02).
- Slides 15–16 (neutrality, plan on one page) become a short closing beat; the open "Decide" items are not in the film (internal discussion points).
